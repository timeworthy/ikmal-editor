What it does

It reads like an editor, not a spell checker.

Typos are the easy part. ikmal editor goes after the padding, the hedging, and the repetition — the things that make writing tiring to read — and it flags them in place, with the fix already written.

Sharper prose

Every suggestion comes with the shorter version.

More than thirty plain-English and conciseness rules, drawn from PlainLanguage.gov and Vale, run alongside the grammar engine. You see what to cut and what to put in its place — one click, and the sentence is fixed.

  • Wordiness, hedging, and padded phrases
  • Repetition and word-family echo across a paragraph
  • Pronouns with no clear antecedent
  • Grammar, agreement, and spelling in 25+ languages

Everywhere you write

One install, and your apps get a proofreader.

ikmal editor runs quietly in your menu bar or tray and answers requests from the tools you already write in. Set it up once and the same rules follow you from email to your notes app to a pull request.

What's inside

Standing on the shoulders of giants.

No gimmicks and no black box. ikmal editor packages battle-tested open-source parts that already work — the engine, models, and rule formats people have relied on for years — wires them together, and takes care of the setup that normally turns running them yourself into a weekend project.

01 · Engine

LanguageTool, running locally

The open-source grammar and spelling engine behind a lot of the checkers you already know, in its official distribution, launched and supervised on your machine instead of in someone's cloud.

25+ languagesofficial build
02 · Language ID

FastText detects what you're writing in

Meta's lid.176.bin model identifies the language of a passage before it is checked, so mixed-language documents get the right rules. Downloaded once, then it lives on your disk.

176 languageson device
03 · Rule packs

Plain English and conciseness rules

More than thirty rules of our own, adapted from PlainLanguage.gov and Vale, that flag padding and hedging. They arrive as ordinary suggestions, so every add-on shows them without knowing anything special.

30+ ruleseditable
04 · Style guides

An importer that turns your guide into rules

PDF, HTML, Markdown, or a text list becomes a native rule set you can switch on per project. Your vocabulary gets checked with the same machinery as grammar.

05 · Quality checks

Repetition and antecedent analysis

A local companion service looks across whole paragraphs for repetition, word-family echo, and pronouns with no clear referent — the sentence-level things a per-sentence checker cannot see.

06 · Optional

A local transformer model, if you want one

A quantized grammar-correction model can run on your machine for harder rewrites. It is opt-in, downloaded only when you ask for it, and it still never sends text anywhere. Skip it and everything else works.

Privacy

“Local-only” with the boring details attached.

Your text is checked by a server running on your own computer, on the loopback address, and nothing leaves it. The one request the app makes on its own is a daily check for a new version — and here is exactly what that sends.

No identifier

No account, no UUID, no hostname, no username, no OS or hardware details. There is no request body and no query string.

One timestamp

A single timestamp is stored on your disk so the check runs at most once every 24 hours. That is the only state it keeps.

Fails silently

It times out after three seconds, runs after the app is already up, and never blocks anything. Download counts come from GitHub, not from the app.

$ikmal-editor -no-update-check
$export IKMAL_EDITOR_NO_UPDATE_CHECK=1

FAQ

Reasonable questions.

Is my writing really never uploaded?
Really. The checker is a server running on your own computer, bound to the loopback address 127.0.0.1. Text goes from your app to that address and back. There is no cloud account and no upload path, so there is nothing to opt out of.
Is it free?
Yes — free and open source under the MIT license. No account, no tiers, no per-seat pricing. If it saves you a subscription, that is the point.
Does it work offline?
Yes, once it has finished setting itself up. The first launch downloads the engine and the language model; after that a plane, a train, or an air-gapped machine is fine.
What do I need installed?
Java 17 or newer, or Docker — the engine runs on the JVM. ikmal editor detects whichever you already have (Homebrew, system Java, or Docker) and will tell you if it finds neither. Roughly 350 MB of disk for the engine and language model.
I already use LanguageTool. Will this break it?
No. ikmal editor is a manager, not a fork: it runs the official LanguageTool distribution and answers on the standard API, so your existing extensions, plug-ins, and settings keep working exactly as they do now. The extra rule packs come through as ordinary suggestions, and you can switch them off.
Which apps does it work with?
Anything with a LanguageTool add-on: Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge, Apple Mail, Microsoft Word, LibreOffice, VS Code, Obsidian, Trilium Notes, and more. You install the add-on you want; ikmal editor then points it at your own machine with one command. It never downloads extensions for you.
Is it affiliated with LanguageTool?
No. It is an independent third-party tool that runs the open-source engine locally. LanguageTool is a registered trademark of LanguageTooler GmbH.
How do I remove it?
One command: ikmal-editor -uninstall. It stops the background service, removes the login item and any browser policy files it wrote, and deletes its folder. Nothing is left behind.
